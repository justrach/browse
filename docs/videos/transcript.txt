Research rarely fits in one tab. In our ten-tab test, browse used fifty-nine percent less RAM than Chrome, leaving more memory for the rest of your work.

Less RAM, more room for research, and potentially lower energy use when reduced memory pressure means less swapping.

Command L brings up search. Pick up a recent query, or explore fresh Google suggestions as you type. Both options are yours to turn on.

Already have a source open? Command K finds your tabs. Type part of the title, choose your page, and jump back into the research.

Compare sources with Command Shift right bracket. Left bracket brings you back, with the highlight following your place.

Within a tab, Command right bracket moves forward in page history. Command left bracket takes you back.

As you scroll, the tab shows how far you have read. Revisit a section, and the progress follows you back.

Codegraff sits beside the page. Ask about what you are reading, explore a question, or get help with a form, without leaving your work.

When it is time to focus, Command S lets the tabs step aside. More room for your research. Browse, for the Mac.
